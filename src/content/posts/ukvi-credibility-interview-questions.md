If you apply for a UK Student visa, there's a good chance someone will want to talk to you first. That **credibility interview** comes either from your university before it issues your CAS (a **pre-CAS interview**) or from UK Visas and Immigration once your visa application is in (a **UKVI interview**). Below are the **UKVI credibility interview questions** that come up most, answer templates you can fill in, and what the interviewer is actually trying to find out.

> **Note:** we rewrote this guide in November 2026 because the old version had sample answers with made-up details. The templates now use placeholders like *[course name]*: fill them with **your own true information**, and never learn someone else's answer by heart.

## Pre-CAS interview vs UKVI interview

| | Pre-CAS interview | UKVI credibility interview |
|---|---|---|
| Who runs it | Your university (or an agent working for it) | UKVI staff, for the Home Office |
| When | Before the university issues your CAS | After you apply for the visa |
| If it goes badly | The university may not issue a CAS | Your visa can be refused |
| Format | Usually a video call | In person, by phone or by video ([University of Reading](https://www.reading.ac.uk/essentials/international/visa-and-immigration/credibility-interviews)) |

The questions overlap heavily, so one round of preparation covers both. Once UKVI invites you, the interview is **compulsory**: skipping it without a good reason gets your application refused, and so does being unable to hold the conversation in English (University of Reading).

## What the interviewer is checking

The immigration rules say a Student visa applicant must be a **genuine student**: someone who really means to take the course, can keep up with it, can afford it, and understands the visa conditions. The interview tests exactly that, through four questions:

1. **Course and university:** do you know what you'll study, and why at this place?
2. **Academic and career logic:** does this course follow sensibly from what you studied and did before?
3. **Money:** do you know where the funds come from and how much you need?
4. **English:** can you follow the questions and reply clearly without a script?

## The questions you are most likely to get

### About the course

- *Why did you choose this course?*
- *What modules will you study? Which one interests you most and why?*
- *How is it assessed? How long is it?*

**How to answer:** pick two or three real modules from the course page and tie them to what you studied before, or to a specific skill you're missing.

> *"I chose the MSc in [course name] because modules like [module 1] and [module 2] build directly on my bachelor's in [subject] at [college], where I worked on [project or topic]. I want to learn [specific skill], which my previous degree did not cover in depth."*

### About the university and the UK

- *Why this university?*
- *Why the UK and not your home country or another country?*
- *Which other universities did you apply to?*

**How to answer:** use reasons someone could *check*: the course content, a placement or project option, the course length, or the cost. "It's famous" or "it ranks well" won't convince anyone.

> *"I compared [university A] and [university B]. I chose [university] because its course includes [specific feature, e.g. an industry project or a particular module], and it is a one-year master's, which keeps my total cost within my family's budget. A similar course in my home country does not offer [specific feature]."*

### About your plans

- *What will you do after the course?*
- *How will this course help your career?*

**How to answer:** describe a realistic plan with a particular kind of job or employer, and be honest. If you hope to get experience on the Graduate Route first, it's fine to say so, as long as you explain where it fits in your long-term plan.

> *"After graduating I want to work as a [job title] in [sector]. Organisations like [type of employer in Nepal] need people with [skill from the course]. In the long term, I plan to [your realistic goal]."*

### About money

- *Who is paying for your studies? What do they do?*
- *How much is your tuition fee? How much have you paid?*
- *How much are living costs where you will study?*

**How to answer:** have your figures ready: the tuition fee, the deposit you've paid, what's left to pay, and the monthly living cost for your city. For visa applications made on or after 30 November 2026, the rules use **£1,570 a month in London and £1,203 a month elsewhere** (see [UK student visa from Nepal](/uk-student-visa-nepal/)). Be able to say who your sponsor is, what they do and where the money is held.

> *"My [father/mother/relative] is sponsoring me. He/she works as a [job] and the funds are in [type of account] at [bank]. My tuition is £[amount]; I have paid a deposit of £[amount], and the remaining £[amount] is due in [month]. I have also shown £[amount] for nine months of living costs in [city]."*

### About your background

- *Tell me about your previous study.*
- *Why is there a gap in your studies?* (if there is one)
- *What did you do after your last degree?*

**How to answer:** explain a gap in a sentence or two, truthfully, and have proof ready: a work letter, a course certificate, or the family reason.

### About your marks and backlogs

- *You had back papers (failed subjects) in your bachelor's. Why?*
- *Your grades are low. How will you cope with a master's?*

**How to answer:** don't try to hide it, because it's on your transcript. Say what went wrong, how you cleared it, and what's different now, whether that's work experience, a recent course or your English score.

### About the UK, the city and your visa conditions

- *Where will you live? How much is your rent?*
- *How many hours can you work during term time?*
- *What will you do if you can't find a part-time job?*
- *Have you ever been refused a visa?*

**How to answer:** know where you'll live and what it costs, and know the work rule: usually **20 hours a week** in term time on a degree course. Your budget has to work **without** any part-time income. If you've been refused a visa before, admit it and explain what has changed, because the interviewer can see your immigration history.

## How to prepare in one week

1. **Go back through your own documents**: offer letter, CAS details, bank statements and personal statement. Everything you say must match them.
2. **Learn the course page**: modules, assessment, length, start date and location.
3. **Memorise your numbers**: tuition, deposit, living costs and your sponsor's income.
4. **Practise out loud** with a friend who asks the questions in a different order each time. Aim for clear answers of three or four sentences.
5. **Record one practice run** and listen for filler words ("um", "like") and answers you rushed.

## On the day

- For an online interview, test your camera, microphone and internet in advance, and find a quiet room.
- Didn't catch a question? Ask: *"Could you repeat the question, please?"* That's perfectly normal.
- Speak clearly at your normal pace. **No one expects a British accent**, only that you understand and can be understood.
- Don't guess or make things up. "I'm not sure, but I believe..." beats a confident wrong answer.

## Red flags to avoid

- Answers that sound rehearsed or lifted from the internet.
- Not knowing your modules or your tuition fee.
- Saying anything that contradicts your documents.
- Giving work as your main reason for coming.

## Frequently asked questions

**What questions are asked in a UKVI credibility interview?** Expect questions on your course and modules, why you picked the university and the UK, your past study and any gaps, who is paying and how much, and what you'll do after the course. The 30 practice questions above cover them.

**What is the difference between a pre-CAS interview and a UKVI interview?** The pre-CAS interview is run by your university before it issues your CAS. The UKVI credibility interview is run by UKVI after you've applied for the visa. The questions are much the same.

**How long does a UKVI credibility interview take?** Usually not long, and it feels more like a conversation, often over video. Your invitation explains the format.

**Do all students get a credibility interview?** No, but prepare as though you will. Lots of universities interview applicants before issuing a CAS, and UKVI can invite anyone.

**Is there a UKVI interview questions and answers PDF?** Not an official one, and answers learned by heart count against you. Use the printable list above with your own true details and save it as a PDF from your browser.

**Can I bring documents to the interview?** Keep them close by, but don't read from notes unless the interviewer asks you to check something.
