Nepal now has a national plan for artificial intelligence. The Cabinet has approved the **National Artificial Intelligence (AI) Policy, 2082** (राष्ट्रिय आर्टिफिसियल इन्टेलिजेन्स (ए.आई.) नीति, २०८२), setting out how the government wants to use AI, regulate it and build the skills to work with it. This guide is based on the **official policy document** published by the Ministry of Communications and Information Technology, with a link to the PDF. Here is what Nepal's AI policy says about the future of artificial intelligence in Nepal, and what it means if you are a student or developer.

> **At a glance**
>
> - **Approved:** by Nepal's Cabinet on 11 August 2025.
> - **Targets:** top 50 in the AI Readiness Index, 5,000 skilled AI professionals and an AI Excellence Centre in every province within five years.
> - **New bodies:** an AI Regulation Council, a National AI Centre (opened November 2025) and AI Excellence Centres.
> - **Official PDF:** [download from the ministry](https://giwmscdnone.gov.np/media/pdf_upload/AI-new_ce2eme0.pdf) (in Nepali).

## Nepal AI Policy 2082 PDF: where to read the official text

The policy is published on the Ministry of Communications and Information Technology's website: [policy page on mocit.gov.np](https://mocit.gov.np/content/13077/national-artifacial-intelligence--aci--policy--2082/), with the [full PDF](https://giwmscdnone.gov.np/media/pdf_upload/AI-new_ce2eme0.pdf) (32 pages, in Nepali). The ministry's policy list also includes an English entry titled "National Artificial Intelligent (AI) Policy, 2082". The targets and institutions below are taken from that official document.

## The basics

- **Approved:** by the Council of Ministers at its meeting on **11 August 2025**. The decision was announced by the Minister for Communications and Information Technology and government spokesperson, Prithvi Subba Gurung ([The Himalayan Times / RSS](https://thehimalayantimes.com/kathmandu/government-endorses-national-ai-policy)).
- **Prepared by:** the Ministry of Communications and Information Technology. A draft was published for comment in February 2025 ([New Business Age](https://newbusinessage.com/news/45072/government-endorses-national-ai-policy)).
- **Time frame:** five years.

## The five targets (section 5 of the policy)

The policy's vision is a "human-centred, ethical and prosperous Nepal" built with AI. Its five targets ([official policy, section 5](https://giwmscdnone.gov.np/media/pdf_upload/AI-new_ce2eme0.pdf)):

1. Use AI to **raise the ICT sector's share of GDP by another 1%**.
2. Bring Nepal **into the top 50 of the AI Readiness Index**.
3. Produce **5,000 skilled people** for the AI sector **within five years**.
4. Set up an **AI Excellence Centre in every province within five years**.
5. Make **everyone, from school students up, AI literate**.

## New institutions: who runs AI policy in Nepal

The policy creates three bodies ([official policy, section 10](https://giwmscdnone.gov.np/media/pdf_upload/AI-new_ce2eme0.pdf)):

- **AI Regulation Council** (ए.आई. नियमन परिषद्): guides, coordinates and regulates the development and use of AI. It is **chaired by the Minister for Communications and Information Technology**. Members include the secretaries of the Prime Minister's Office, the communications, finance and education ministries, the chair of the Nepal Telecommunications Authority, the head of the e-Governance Board, and three experts from the private sector and universities (at least one a woman). It meets at least twice a year.
- **National AI Centre** (राष्ट्रिय ए.आई. केन्द्र): set up under the communications ministry to promote and regulate AI research and use, act as Nepal's international focal point on AI, set standards for AI products and services, publish a **National AI Index**, and run a **national AI portal**. It also serves as the council's secretariat. Minister Jagadish Kharel **inaugurated the centre on 10 November 2025**, starting "from a small room in the ministry" ([The Annapurna Express, 11 Nov 2025](https://theannapurnaexpress.com/story/59899)).
- **AI Excellence Centres**: in universities and research institutions, for AI study, research and development.

Reporting on the approved policy also mentions **AI incubation hubs** for start-ups and a **"brain gain" programme** to attract Nepali AI professionals working abroad ([The Annapurna Express](https://theannapurnaexpress.com/story/57603)).

## Where AI is meant to be used

| Sector | Examples named in the policy |
|---|---|
| Education | AI literacy from school students to the general public |
| Agriculture | production forecasting, soil monitoring, pest and disease prediction, irrigation |
| Health | diagnostic imaging, early disease detection, genomic analysis |
| Energy | smart grids, smart switching, smart meters |
| Transport | traffic flow, parking, public transit, road safety |
| Tourism and finance | named for AI integration |

## Rules, data and safety

The policy promises new **data protection laws** and updates to existing ones, **regulatory sandboxes** for testing AI systems safely, **standards and certification** for AI applications, and guidelines against misuse such as **misinformation** and **intellectual property violations** (The Annapurna Express).

**Is there an AI law in Nepal?** Not yet. The policy says the government "will make the necessary legal arrangements" to implement it, and that sector-specific AI directives and guidelines will be based on it. Provinces and local governments can adapt it to their own needs ([official policy, section 11](https://giwmscdnone.gov.np/media/pdf_upload/AI-new_ce2eme0.pdf)).

On infrastructure, it mentions data centres, cloud and high-performance computing, reliable electricity, **green data centres in mountain regions**, and a feasibility study on producing AI-related hardware locally.

## What critics say is missing

Commentators reviewing the policy point to gaps that matter for whether it works:

- **No detailed roadmap**: few concrete timelines, budgets or measures of success for each goal ([Sunway College, on the draft](https://sunway.edu.np/nepal-ai-policy-whats-missing-next/)).
- **Infrastructure first**: reliable power, internet and computing are still weak outside the big cities (Sunway College).
- **Data rules**: data protection and AI rules are promised, but not yet written into law (official policy, section 11).
- **Money and accountability**: whether institutions will be funded and held to account ([The Himalayan Times, opinion](https://thehimalayantimes.com/opinion/nepals-ai-policy-exists-but-is-nepal-ready)).

One early criticism is out of date: the **National AI Centre now exists** (opened 10 November 2025).

## The big question: implementation

Writing a policy is the easy part. The National AI Centre now exists, but whether it is staffed and funded, and whether the provincial excellence centres follow, is the thing to watch.

> **Update (1 November 2026):** Commentators have since asked whether the policy will be backed by enough money, strong institutions and real accountability ([The Himalayan Times, opinion](https://thehimalayantimes.com/opinion/nepals-ai-policy-exists-but-is-nepal-ready)). Nepal has also changed government twice since this policy was approved: the interim government formed after the September 2025 protests, and the [RSP government of Prime Minister Balen Shah](/nepal-gen-z-protests-to-balen-shah-government-explained/) elected in March 2026. AI training is also spreading, from the "AI for Nepal 2026" school campaign launched in Dhangadhi ([ICT Frame](https://ictframe.com/ai-for-nepal-kicks-off-in-dhangadhi/)) to free online courses from the National Vocational Training Establishment ([Nepal News](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/main-left/nepal-news-evening-briefing-sunday-november-01-2026/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**When was Nepal's National AI Policy approved?** The Council of Ministers approved it on 11 August 2025 (26 Shrawan 2082). The ministry published it on 3 Bhadra 2082 (19 August 2025).

**Where can I download the Nepal AI Policy 2082 PDF?** From the Ministry of Communications and Information Technology: [policy page](https://mocit.gov.np/content/13077/national-artifacial-intelligence--aci--policy--2082/) or the [direct PDF link](https://giwmscdnone.gov.np/media/pdf_upload/AI-new_ce2eme0.pdf).

**What is the main goal of Nepal's AI policy?** To use AI for economic and social development: top 50 in the AI Readiness Index, 5,000 skilled AI professionals and an AI Excellence Centre in every province within five years.

**Who regulates AI in Nepal?** The policy creates an AI Regulation Council chaired by the Minister for Communications and Information Technology, with the National AI Centre as its secretariat.

**Is there a National AI Centre in Nepal?** Yes. It was inaugurated on 10 November 2025 inside the Ministry of Communications and Information Technology.
