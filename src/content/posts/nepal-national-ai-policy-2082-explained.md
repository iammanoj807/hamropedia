Nepal now has a national plan for artificial intelligence. The Cabinet has approved the **National Artificial Intelligence (AI) Policy, 2082**, setting out how the government wants to use AI, regulate it and build the skills to work with it. Here is what it says, and what it means if you are a student or developer in Nepal.

## The basics

- **Approved:** by the Council of Ministers at its meeting on **11 August 2025**. The decision was announced by the Minister for Communications and Information Technology and government spokesperson, Prithvi Subba Gurung ([The Himalayan Times / RSS](https://thehimalayantimes.com/kathmandu/government-endorses-national-ai-policy)).
- **Prepared by:** the Ministry of Communications and Information Technology. A draft was published for comment in February 2025 ([New Business Age](https://newbusinessage.com/news/45072/government-endorses-national-ai-policy)).
- **Time frame:** five years.

## The headline targets

According to reporting on the approved policy by [The Annapurna Express](https://theannapurnaexpress.com/story/57603), the policy aims to:

- put Nepal among the **top 50 countries in the global AI Readiness Index**;
- **add 1% to GDP** from the ICT sector;
- develop skilled people and AI-driven innovation across key sectors within five years.

## New institutions

- a **National AI Center** to oversee implementation;
- **AI Centers of Excellence in every province**;
- a **National AI Index** to track progress;
- **AI incubation hubs** for start-ups;
- a **"brain gain" programme** to attract Nepali AI professionals working abroad.

## Where AI is meant to be used

| Sector | Examples named in the policy |
|---|---|
| Education | AI literacy from school students to the general public |
| Agriculture | production forecasting, soil monitoring, pest and disease prediction, irrigation |
| Health | diagnostic imaging, early disease detection, genomic analysis |
| Energy | smart grids, smart switching, smart meters |
| Transport | traffic flow, parking, public transit, road safety |
| Tourism and finance | named for AI integration |

## Rules, data and safety

The policy promises new **data protection laws** and updates to existing ones, **regulatory sandboxes** for testing AI systems safely, **standards and certification** for AI applications, and guidelines against misuse such as **misinformation** and **intellectual property violations** (The Annapurna Express).

On infrastructure, it mentions data centres, cloud and high-performance computing, reliable electricity, **green data centres in mountain regions**, and a feasibility study on producing AI-related hardware locally.

## The big question: implementation

Writing a policy is the easy part. Commentators have asked whether it will be backed by enough money, strong institutions and real accountability ([The Himalayan Times, opinion](https://thehimalayantimes.com/opinion/nepals-ai-policy-exists-but-is-nepal-ready)). Whether the AI Center and the provincial centres are actually set up and funded is the thing to watch.

> **Update (1 November 2026):** Nepal has changed government twice since this policy was approved: the interim government formed after the September 2025 protests, and the [RSP government of Prime Minister Balen Shah](/nepal-gen-z-protests-to-balen-shah-government-explained/) elected in March 2026. AI training is spreading in the meantime, from school campaigns to free government courses; see [AI training in Nepal: what's on in late 2026](/ai-training-in-nepal-2026/).

## What it means for you

If you are a student or a developer in Nepal, the direction is clear: the government expects demand for people who can **build, evaluate and apply AI**, in agriculture, health and energy as much as in software companies. You don't need to wait for a national centre to start:

- Learn the foundations for free with the [Hamropedia AI & ML roadmap](/ai-roadmap/): 8 phases, from Python and maths to LLMs and AI engineering.
- Read [how to learn AI and machine learning for free](/how-to-learn-ai-and-machine-learning-for-free/) for a realistic plan.
- Build something local: a Nepali text classifier, a crop-disease image model, a Devanagari handwriting recogniser. Projects with Nepali data stand out.
