The **Graduate visa UK** (the post-study work visa, which many students call **PSW**) is being cut from 2 years to **18 months** for applications made from **1 January 2027** ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/graduate-visa)). But the government has partly reversed the cut: in its **Budget on 28 October 2026**, it said graduates of **"high-compliance universities"** will keep **two years**. Here is who gets 18 months, who still gets 2 years, and what to do now.

> **At a glance**
>
> - **Apply on or before 31 December 2026:** **2 years**, whatever your university.
> - **Apply from 1 January 2027:** **18 months** for most bachelor's and master's graduates.
> - **Exception (announced, not yet law):** graduates of "high-compliance" universities keep **2 years**. No list yet.
> - **PhD graduates:** still **3 years**.
> - **Cost:** £937 application fee plus the health surcharge.

## Is the UK Graduate visa 18 months now?

Not yet. gov.uk says a Graduate visa lasts **"2 years if you apply on or before 31 December 2026"** and **"18 months if you apply on or after 1 January 2027"**. Doctoral graduates get 3 years ([gov.uk: Graduate visa](https://www.gov.uk/graduate-visa)). What counts is the date you **apply**, not the date you graduate or the date your course started.

## Is the Graduate visa the same as PSW?

In everyday talk, yes. Students and agents in Nepal often call the Graduate visa **PSW** (post-study work), but gov.uk never uses that name. The old **Tier 1 (Post-Study Work)** visa closed to new applicants on **6 April 2012** ([Home Office inspection report, PDF](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a8093aced915d74e33fb372/An-Inspection-of-Tier-1-PBS-Investor-and-Entrepreneur-Applications_Sept_2013.pdf)). The **Graduate route** that students now call PSW opened on **1 July 2021** ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/graduate-route-for-best-and-brightest-launches)). Despite the nickname, you don't have to work on it: the Migration Advisory Committee notes there is "no requirement to undertake any form of work" ([MAC review, PDF](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6745b2eecdd295aea88098b6/MAC+Rapid+Review+of+Graduate+Route+-+FINAL+Nov+24__1_.pdf)).

## How to apply for the Graduate visa

1. **Wait until your university has reported that you completed your course.** You can't apply before that.
2. **Apply online from inside the UK**, while your Student visa is still valid ([gov.uk: apply](https://www.gov.uk/graduate-visa/apply)).
3. **Prove your identity**, either with the **UK Immigration: ID Check app** (and a UKVI account) or by giving fingerprints and a photo at a UKVCAS service point.
4. **Pay the £937 fee and the health surcharge.**
5. **Don't travel** outside the UK, Ireland, the Channel Islands or the Isle of Man until you get a decision, or your application is withdrawn.
6. **Decision:** usually within 8 weeks. If approved, you get an **eVisa** (a digital record of your status).

## What the Budget said

The Budget states that *"the graduate visa duration will be set at two years for individuals who graduate from high-compliance universities"* where there are *"strong labour market outcomes"*, describing it as an extension "from 18 months to two years for eligible universities" ([The PIE News, 28 Oct 2026](https://thepienews.com/breaking-uk-to-extend-graduate-visa-to-two-years-for-graduates-of-high-compliance-universities/)).

## Who gets what

| Situation | Graduate visa length |
|---|---|
| You apply **on or before 31 December 2026** | **2 years** (the current rule) |
| You apply from 1 January 2027, from a **qualifying high-compliance university** | **2 years** (new Budget policy, details to come) |
| You apply from 1 January 2027, from **other universities** | **18 months** (the cut announced in October 2025) |
| PhD and other doctoral graduates | 3 years (unchanged) |

The 18-month rule and the 1 January 2027 cut-off come from the Home Office's October 2025 rule changes ([UKCISA](https://www.ukcisa.org.uk/news/student-update-changes-to-the-student-and-graduate-rules/)) and are now on [gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/graduate-visa).

## Graduate visa UK: rules, cost and eligibility

From the official gov.uk page ([Graduate visa](https://www.gov.uk/graduate-visa)):

- **Where you apply:** you must be **in the UK** when you apply, on a Student visa (or Tier 4).
- **Course completion:** your university must have **told the Home Office you have successfully completed your course** before you can apply. This is the most common reason people can't apply on time.
- **Fee:** **£937**, plus the health surcharge, usually **£1,035 for each year**. For 18 months that is £1,035 + £517.50 = **£1,552.50**; for 2 years, £2,070 ([gov.uk: healthcare surcharge](https://www.gov.uk/healthcare-immigration-application/how-much-pay)).
- **Decision:** usually within **8 weeks**.
- **No extension:** you **cannot extend** a Graduate visa. To stay longer you must switch to another visa, such as a Skilled Worker visa.

## What you can and cannot do on a Graduate visa

You **can** work in most jobs, look for work, be self-employed, do voluntary work, travel abroad and return, and keep living in the UK with your partner and children. You **cannot** claim most benefits (public funds) or the State Pension, or work as a professional sportsperson. You can only study on a course that isn't eligible for a Student visa ([gov.uk: Graduate visa](https://www.gov.uk/graduate-visa)).

You **don't need a job offer or a sponsor**, and you can stay in the UK while you wait for a decision.

## Can my partner and children stay?

Only family who are **already your dependants**:

- **Partner:** must have been your dependant on your Student visa, and still be your partner (married, in a civil partnership, or together for at least 2 years).
- **Children under 18:** usually must have been your dependants on your Student visa, unless they were **born in the UK** while you held your Student or Graduate visa.
- **New dependants can't join you from abroad** on a Graduate visa. They may be able to if you switch to another visa, such as a Skilled Worker visa ([gov.uk: partner and children](https://www.gov.uk/graduate-visa/your-partner-and-children)).

## What is not decided yet

This is **policy intent, not yet a rule**. The Budget did not define:

- what a **"high-compliance" university** is;
- what counts as **"strong labour market outcomes"**;
- **which universities** will qualify, or how many.

The Home Office and the Department for Education will design the details, which are expected to be announced "in due course" (The PIE News). Until the change appears in the Immigration Rules on gov.uk, nobody can tell you for certain whether your university qualifies, so be wary of consultancies that claim to know.

## How universities reacted

- **Russell Group** (Hollie Chandler, director of strategic policy): avoiding the planned cut for highly compliant universities "will help protect our reputation as a global study destination."
- **Universities UK** (Vivienne Stern, chief executive): a "welcome recognition" of what international students bring, adding that "the rules need to be stable and fair".

## What students should do now

1. **If you finish your course in 2026, apply on or before 31 December 2026** if you can. You get two years whatever your university. Ask your university when it will report your completion to the Home Office, because you can't apply before that.
2. **Choosing a university for 2027 or later?** Watch for the official list of qualifying universities before you decide, and don't rely on rankings alone.
3. **Check the official source.** Follow [gov.uk: Graduate visa](https://www.gov.uk/graduate-visa) and your university's international student advice team.

The Budget also said the **High Potential Individual (HPI) visa** will be opened to graduates of the **top 150 global institutions**, with a larger cap (The PIE News).

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I switch from a Graduate visa to a Skilled Worker visa?** Yes, from inside the UK, if you have a job offer from a licensed sponsor that meets the Skilled Worker job and salary rules. The Graduate visa is not on gov.uk's list of visas you can't switch from ([gov.uk: switch to a Skilled Worker visa](https://www.gov.uk/skilled-worker-visa/switch-to-this-visa)).

**Does the Graduate visa count towards ILR?** Not under the new Earned Settlement rules: time on Student and Graduate visas will not count, and the 10-year Long Residence route, where it used to count, will close. See [ILR new rules](/ilr-changes-uk-5-year-vs-10-year-route-earned-settlement/).

For all the current UK student visa numbers (fees, funds, dependants), see [UK student visa rules explained](/uk-student-visa-from-nepal/). Planning to stay long term? Graduate visa time will not count towards settlement under the new rules: see [UK ILR changes: 5-year vs 10-year routes](/ilr-changes-uk-5-year-vs-10-year-route-earned-settlement/).
