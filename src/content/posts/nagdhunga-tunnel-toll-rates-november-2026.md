Vehicles using the **Nagdhunga tunnel**, Nepal's first road tunnel on the main route between Kathmandu and the west, **pay a toll from 1 November 2026**. Earlier notices had said 31 October; the latest reports confirm 1 November ([Makalu Khabar](https://english.makalukhabar.com/nagdhunga-tunnel-road-toll-collection-begins/); [Meroauto](https://www.en.meroauto.com/nagdhunga-tunnel-road-introduces-toll-charges-from-today/)).

> **At a glance**
>
> - **From:** 1 November 2026.
> - **Car, jeep or van:** Rs 65 into Kathmandu, Rs 60 out.
> - **Not allowed:** motorcycles, three-wheelers, and vehicles carrying petrol, diesel or LPG.
> - **Pay with:** N-Tag/RFID or QR (cash was also reported in the first phase).

## Toll rates

| Vehicle | Entering Kathmandu | Leaving Kathmandu |
|---|---|---|
| Car, van, jeep, pickup, microbus, other light vehicles | **Rs 65** | **Rs 60** |
| Minibus, mini truck | Rs 115 | Rs 80 |
| Bus, single-rear-axle truck | Rs 260 | Rs 200 |
| Heavy vehicles | Rs 600 | Rs 250 |

Rates as reported by [Desh Sanchar](https://english.deshsanchar.com/nagdhunga-tunnel-toll-fees-announced-for-vehicles-traveling-to-kathmandu-dhading/), [Himal Press](https://en.himalpress.com/govt-sets-toll-fee-for-vehicles-using-nagdhunga-naubise-tunnel/) and The Kathmandu Post.

## Who cannot use the tunnel

**Motorcycles and scooters, three-wheelers (tempos, autorickshaws), pedestrians and other non-motorised vehicles** are not allowed, and nor are vehicles carrying flammable or explosive goods such as **petrol, diesel and LPG** ([The Kathmandu Post](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2026/07/01/nagdhunga-sisnekhola-tunnelway-completed-after-79-months-set-to-open-within-weeks)). These vehicles keep using the old road over Nagdhunga.

## How to pay

The tunnel is designed for **digital tolls**: N-Tag stickers linked to a bank account, RFID tags, and QR payments (such as Fonepay) for occasional users (The Kathmandu Post). Reports on the first days differ: one says **cash and QR** are accepted during the initial phase, with RFID tags to follow (Makalu Khabar); another describes N-Tag as the main method (Meroauto). If you drive the route often, an N-Tag will probably save time.

## The tunnel in numbers

- **Main tunnel:** 2,688 m, with a parallel **2,557 m emergency escape tunnel**.
- **Approach roads:** 2.8 km, linking Nagdhunga (Kathmandu side) and Sisnekhola (Dhading side).
- **Money:** about **Rs 21.5 billion** in concessional loans from Japan, plus about **Rs 6 billion** from the Government of Nepal.
- **Built by:** Japan's Hazama Ando Corporation, which handed it over in May 2026. It is run by the Yusin-ART joint venture.
- **Time taken:** work began on **21 October 2019** with a 42-month deadline; it took **79 months**, delayed by Covid-19 and local obstructions.

(All from [The Kathmandu Post, 1 July 2026](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2026/07/01/nagdhunga-sisnekhola-tunnelway-completed-after-79-months-set-to-open-within-weeks).)

## Why it matters

The road over Nagdhunga is one of the main gateways into the Kathmandu Valley for traffic from Pokhara, Chitwan and the Birgunj border, and the steep pass is known for traffic jams. A tunnel with an emergency escape route and round-the-clock traffic management is meant to make the journey smoother and safer.
