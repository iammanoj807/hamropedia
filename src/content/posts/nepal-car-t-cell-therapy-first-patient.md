Nepal has started **CAR T-cell therapy**, one of the most advanced cancer treatments in the world, and the cells are being **made in Nepal**. The first patient, a **20-year-old woman with relapsed acute lymphoblastic leukaemia**, was treated at **Tribhuvan University Teaching Hospital (TUTH)** in Maharajgunj, Kathmandu ([The Kathmandu Post, 12 October 2026](https://kathmandupost.com/health/2026/10/12/nepal-begins-car-t-cell-therapy-for-cancer-patients)).

"Her condition is stable, and doctors attending to her are monitoring her response to the treatment," said **Dr Anjan Shrestha**, the study's clinical lead.

## What CAR T-cell therapy is

CAR T-cell therapy uses the patient's own immune cells as the medicine. Doctors collect the patient's **T cells** (a type of white blood cell), and a lab reprograms them to recognise a marker on cancer cells. The Nepali product targets **CD19**, a protein found on certain blood cancer cells. The cells are multiplied and given back to the patient, where they hunt down the cancer.

## Made in Nepal

- The cells are produced by the **Centre for Regenerative Medicine Nepal (CRM Nepal)**, based in Kathmandu.
- Nepal's **Department of Drug Administration approved** the locally developed CD19 CAR T-cell product for clinical use in **March 2026**.
- Preclinical research began in **2022**, according to **Dr Prashun Upadhyaya**, who co-administered the treatment.

(The Kathmandu Post)

## Who can get it

It is not a first treatment. Patients first receive standard treatment such as chemotherapy; CAR T-cell therapy is considered **only if that fails or the cancer comes back**. For now it is available only at TUTH, and doctors say more patients will be treated and other hospitals will start soon (The Kathmandu Post).

## Why it matters

Until now, Nepali patients who needed this treatment had to go abroad. Dr Shivaji Poudel, a former director of BP Koirala Memorial Cancer Hospital, said it costs **more than Rs 7 million per patient in India**, and treatment in India and China can cost tens of millions of rupees. "This is really a noble and effective technology for cancer treatment," he said. A locally made product could put it within reach of far more families.
