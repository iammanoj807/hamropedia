Plainclothes police went into the offices of several media outlets in Kathmandu on **30 October 2026** and took journalists for questioning over news reports, drawing strong criticism from journalists' organisations and political parties.

## What happened

- Police visited several newsrooms. The **Press Council Nepal** expressed serious concern at reports that plainclothes officers had entered newsrooms, detained journalists and tried to arrest others ([OnlineKhabar](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/police-visit-offices-of-multiple-media-outlets-several-journalists-taken-into-custody.html); [Republica](https://myrepublica.nagariknetwork.com/news/police-detain-journalists-in-newsroom-raids-over-news-reports-89-32.html)).
- A **Nepal Press** reporter was taken from his office for questioning about a report that the government was seeking to remove the Inspector General of Police, **Dan Bahadur Karki** ([Nepal News](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/nation/police-arrest-two-journalists-including-nepal-press-reporter/)).
- The action followed reports in several newspapers and news sites the day before that Prime Minister Balen Shah was seeking the police chief's resignation ([ANI](https://www.aninews.in/news/world/asia/nepal-police-rounds-up-journalists-over-their-coverage-of-alleged-whatsapp-chat-between-convict-and-high-profile-officials20261030210318/); [ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/international/nepal-police-detain-three-journalists-media-body-protests-enn26103107035)).

Reports differ on **how many** journalists were involved (from two or three to "several").

## Two different accounts

- **Journalists' bodies:** the **Federation of Nepali Journalists (FNJ)** called the police action "unconstitutional" and held a protest at **Maitighar on 31 October** ([Peoples' Review](https://peoplesreview.com.np/2026/10/31/journalists-stage-protest-at-maitighar-against-police-raids-on-media-houses-and-arrests/)). Critics said the aim was to pressure reporters into revealing their sources ([Nepali Times](https://nepalitimes.com/nepal-detains-journalists-to-warn-rest-of-free-press)).
- **Police:** Nepal Police said the journalists were **not detained** but called in by its Cyber Bureau for **routine questioning** about the accuracy and factual basis of the reports. The Prime Minister's Office reportedly denied involvement.

## Political reaction

- The ruling **Rastriya Swatantra Party (RSP)** directed the government to investigate the incidents. Its spokesperson, Jagadish Kharel, said the party would not compromise on press freedom ([Nepal News, 31 Oct](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/main-right/nepal-news-evening-briefing-saturday-october-31-2026/)).
- **Nepali Congress** president **Gagan Thapa** said a list of **28 journalists** had reportedly been sent to police and demanded an explanation from the government. The **Nepal Gen Z Front** called for a parliamentary inquiry ([Nepal News, 1 Nov](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/main-left/nepal-news-evening-briefing-sunday-november-01-2026/)).

## Why it matters

Nepal's constitution guarantees press freedom and bars prior censorship, and protecting sources is a basic principle of journalism. How the government responds to these incidents will be an early test of a party that came to power on the back of a youth movement against censorship: the September 2025 protests began with a social media ban.
