Plainclothes police went to the offices of **at least five media outlets in Kathmandu on 30 October 2026**, taking some journalists into custody for questioning and asking for others, in connection with published news reports.

## What happened

According to [OnlineKhabar](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/police-visit-offices-of-multiple-media-outlets-several-journalists-taken-into-custody.html):

- **Nepal Press:** journalist **Bishwa Mani Subedi** was taken into custody.
- **Motivate News:** **Shobharaj Baral** was detained after plainclothes officers came to the office in Bijulibazar.
- **DNA Khabar:** editor-in-chief **Dhiraj Basnet** was taken in after a call from the Kathmandu Valley Crime Investigation Office about "an alleged eSewa scam".
- **Diyo Post:** four plainclothes officers came to the Anamnagar office asking for the editor-in-chief and the news coordinator for a "conversation".
- **Naya Patrika:** five plainclothes officers came looking for a correspondent; the editor said they "had not presented an arrest warrant".

## Which reports were involved

The reports differ from outlet to outlet:

- **Nepal Press:** Subedi was held, "on the basis of a letter from the Cyber Bureau", for questioning about a Nepal Press report claiming the government was seeking to remove Inspector General of Police **Dan Bahadur Karki** ([Nepal News](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/nation/police-arrest-two-journalists-including-nepal-press-reporter/)).
- **Diyo Post** had reported receiving threats over a story concerning the chief justice (OnlineKhabar).
- **ANI** described the police action as linked to coverage of an alleged WhatsApp chat between a convict and high-profile officials ([ANI](https://www.aninews.in/news/world/asia/nepal-police-rounds-up-journalists-over-their-coverage-of-alleged-whatsapp-chat-between-convict-and-high-profile-officials20261030210318/)).

The **Press Council Nepal** expressed serious concern at reports that plainclothes police had entered newsrooms and detained journalists ([Republica](https://myrepublica.nagariknetwork.com/news/police-detain-journalists-in-newsroom-raids-over-news-reports-89-32.html)).

## Update: police response and reaction

> **Update (1 November 2026):** the following happened after 30 October.

- **Police:** Nepal Police said on 31 October that the journalists were **not detained** but called in by its Cyber Bureau for **routine questioning** about the accuracy and factual basis of their reports. The Prime Minister's Office reportedly denied involvement.
- **Journalists:** the **Federation of Nepali Journalists (FNJ)** called the police action "unconstitutional" and held a protest at **Maitighar on 31 October** ([Peoples' Review](https://peoplesreview.com.np/2026/10/31/journalists-stage-protest-at-maitighar-against-police-raids-on-media-houses-and-arrests/)). Critics said the aim was to pressure reporters into revealing their sources ([Nepali Times](https://nepalitimes.com/nepal-detains-journalists-to-warn-rest-of-free-press)).
- **Ruling party:** the **Rastriya Swatantra Party (RSP)** directed the government to investigate the incidents. Its spokesperson, Jagadish Kharel, said the party would not compromise on press freedom ([Nepal News, 31 Oct](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/main-right/nepal-news-evening-briefing-saturday-october-31-2026/)).
- **Opposition:** Nepali Congress president **Gagan Thapa** said a list of **28 journalists** had reportedly been sent to police and demanded an explanation; the **Nepal Gen Z Front** called for a parliamentary inquiry ([Nepal News, 1 Nov](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/main-left/nepal-news-evening-briefing-sunday-november-01-2026/)).

## Why it matters

Nepal's constitution guarantees press freedom and bars prior censorship, and protecting sources is a basic principle of journalism. How the government responds will be an early test of a party that came to power on the back of a youth movement against censorship: the September 2025 protests began with a social media ban.
