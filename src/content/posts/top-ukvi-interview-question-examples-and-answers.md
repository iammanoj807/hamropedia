Many students applying for a UK Student visa are asked to take a **credibility interview**, either by their university before it issues a CAS, or by UK Visas and Immigration (UKVI) during the visa application. This guide explains what the interview is really checking and how to prepare honest, specific answers.

> **Update:** This article was rewritten in November 2026. The earlier version contained sample answers with invented details. The templates below use placeholders such as *[course name]* for you to fill with **your own true information**. Never memorise someone else's answer.

## What the interview is checking

UK immigration rules require Student visa applicants to be **genuine students**: people who really intend to study the course, can follow it, can pay for it, and understand the conditions of their visa. The interview is how that is checked. Interviewers mainly look at four things:

1. **Your course and university choice**: do you know what you will study, and why here?
2. **Your academic and career logic**: does this course make sense after your previous study and work?
3. **Your finances**: do you understand where the money comes from, and how much you need?
4. **Your English**: can you understand and answer clearly without a script?

It is usually short and conversational, often by video call. Your university or the visa application centre will tell you the format.

## The questions you are most likely to get

### About the course

- *Why did you choose this course?*
- *What modules will you study? Which one interests you most and why?*
- *How is it assessed? How long is it?*

**How to answer:** name two or three real modules from the course page and connect them to your past study or a specific skill you want.

> *"I chose the MSc in [course name] because modules like [module 1] and [module 2] build directly on my bachelor's in [subject] at [college], where I worked on [project or topic]. I want to learn [specific skill], which my previous degree did not cover in depth."*

### About the university and the UK

- *Why this university?*
- *Why the UK and not your home country or another country?*
- *Which other universities did you apply to?*

**How to answer:** give *checkable* reasons, such as course content, a placement or project option, the length of the course, or the cost, not "it is famous" or "it has a good ranking".

> *"I compared [university A] and [university B]. I chose [university] because its course includes [specific feature, e.g. an industry project or a particular module], and it is a one-year master's, which keeps my total cost within my family's budget. A similar course in my home country does not offer [specific feature]."*

### About your plans

- *What will you do after the course?*
- *How will this course help your career?*

**How to answer:** describe a realistic plan with a specific kind of role or employer. Be honest. If you want to use the Graduate Route to gain experience first, you can say so, and explain how it fits your long-term plan.

> *"After graduating I want to work as a [job title] in [sector]. Organisations like [type of employer in Nepal] need people with [skill from the course]. In the long term, I plan to [your realistic goal]."*

### About money

- *Who is paying for your studies? What do they do?*
- *How much is your tuition fee? How much have you paid?*
- *How much are living costs where you will study?*

**How to answer:** know your numbers exactly: tuition fee, deposit paid, remaining balance, and the monthly living cost for your city (the visa rules use **£1,529/month in London and £1,171/month elsewhere**; see [UK student visa rules](/uk-immigration-2025-new-student-visa-rules-explained-simply/)). Know who your sponsor is, what they do, and where the funds are held.

> *"My [father/mother/relative] is sponsoring me. He/she works as a [job] and the funds are in [type of account] at [bank]. My tuition is £[amount]; I have paid a deposit of £[amount], and the remaining £[amount] is due in [month]. I have also shown £[amount] for nine months of living costs in [city]."*

### About your background

- *Tell me about your previous study.*
- *Why is there a gap in your studies?* (if there is one)
- *What did you do after your last degree?*

**How to answer:** explain any gap briefly and truthfully, with evidence you can show (work letter, course certificate, family reason).

## How to prepare in one week

1. **Re-read your own documents**: offer letter, CAS details, bank statements, personal statement. Your answers must match them.
2. **Study the course page**: modules, assessment, length, start date, location.
3. **Know your numbers**: tuition, deposit, living costs, your sponsor's income.
4. **Practise out loud** with a friend asking questions in a different order each time. Aim for clear answers of three or four sentences.
5. **Record yourself** once and listen back for filler words ("um", "like") and rushed answers.

## On the day

- Test your camera, microphone and internet beforehand if it is online, and sit somewhere quiet.
- Keep your documents nearby, but don't read from notes.
- If you don't understand a question, ask: *"Could you repeat the question, please?"* That is completely fine.
- Speak clearly and at a normal pace. **Nobody expects a British accent**; they expect you to understand and be understood.
- Never guess or invent. "I'm not sure, but I believe..." is better than a confident wrong answer.

## Red flags to avoid

- Answers that sound memorised or copied from the internet.
- Not knowing your course modules or your tuition fee.
- Contradicting your documents.
- Saying your main reason for coming is to work.
