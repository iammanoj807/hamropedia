The **ILR new rules** are now confirmed, and they are the biggest shake-up of **indefinite leave to remain (ILR)** in years. The Home Office published its final **"Earned Settlement"** plan on **2 November 2026**. For most people the normal wait for settlement goes from **5 years to 10 years**. High earners, key public service workers and people with a British partner keep shorter routes, while people who claim benefits or break immigration rules face longer ones ([Home Office, Government Response, Nov 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6ae89fddf4a6b3f59cf37580/FINAL_VERSION_-_Earned_Settlement_-_Consultation_Response.pdf)). None of this is in force yet: the first changes arrive in the **December Immigration Rules**, and the rest follow during **2027**.

## What is indefinite leave to remain (ILR)?

**ILR (indefinite leave to remain)**, often just called **settlement**, means you can live in the UK permanently: no visa renewals, no work restrictions, and access to public funds. It is normally the step you take before applying for **British citizenship** ([gov.uk: Settle in the UK](https://www.gov.uk/settle-in-the-uk)).

Under the current rules, people on routes such as the **Skilled Worker visa** can usually apply after **5 years**, and anyone with **10 years of continuous lawful residence** can apply through the **Long Residence** route ([gov.uk: Long residence](https://www.gov.uk/long-residence)). Earned Settlement changes both.

### "ILR 10 years" means two different things

Search for "ILR 10 years" and you'll find two separate rules muddled together:

- **The old 10-year Long Residence route** (gov.uk's [long residence](https://www.gov.uk/long-residence) page): settlement after 10 years of continuous lawful residence on **any combination of visas**, Student and Graduate years included. It still exists today, but **it will close** under Earned Settlement.
- **The new 10-year standard qualifying period**: the default wait on routes such as the Skilled Worker visa, raised from 5 years, where **only time on a qualifying route counts**.

In other words, a decade of study followed by work won't be enough by itself any more. Only the years you spend on a route that leads to settlement count.

## ILR new rules: the 3, 5, 10 and 20-year routes

| How long | Who it is for | What you need |
|---|---|---|
| **3 years** | Exceptional economic contribution | At least **£125,141** in 3 of the last 4 years, or entry through the "Highest Talent Group" (still under review) |
| **5 years** | Strong contribution | At least **£50,271** in 3 of the last 5 years, **or** 5 years in an eligible public service job, **or** a British connection |
| **10 years** | The new standard route | At least **£12,571** in 5 of the 7 years before applying, plus all the basic requirements |
| **20 years** | People shut out of the shorter routes | For example, claiming specified public funds in the last 5 years, or breaking immigration rules as an adult |

(Home Office, Table 1)

Each threshold matches a UK income tax band (£12,571 is where basic-rate tax begins, £50,271 the higher rate, £125,141 the additional rate), so the figures will shift if the bands do.

## Who still gets ILR after 5 years

- **Higher earners:** **£50,271** or more a year in 3 of the 5 years before you apply. Two weaker years are tolerated, for instance because of maternity leave or illness. These 3- and 5-year earnings routes cover the **Skilled Worker, Global Talent, Scale-up, Innovator Founder** and **International Sportsperson** routes.
- **Health and education public service workers:** eligible jobs paid on **national pay scales**, such as **doctors, nurses and teachers**. Annex C of the Home Office response has the full list.
- **People with a British connection:** eligible **partners, parents and children of British citizens**, plus **UK Ancestry** visa holders.
- **Groups already promised a route:** **Hong Kong BN(O)** visa holders stay on 5 years. **HM Armed Forces**, including **Gurkha** and Hong Kong military unit veterans discharged before 1 July 1997, keep what they have now: service personnel qualify after at least 4 years' service, and their partners and children after 5 years.

### Transitional protection for some people already in the UK

This protection covers only people who were already on the route **before the Immigration White Paper of May 2025**:

- **Care workers** who entered or switched into the care worker route before May 2025 keep **5 years**, as long as they meet the **£25,000** salary, sponsorship and work conditions. Anyone who falls short can apply for another **five-year Health and Care visa** and try again.
- **Health and education workers below degree level (RQF 6)** on national pay scales, such as **nursing auxiliaries and teaching assistants**, keep a **5-year** route.

(Home Office, Chapter 8)

## Who moves to 10 years

Nearly everyone else on a settlement route, including:

- **Skilled Workers** on **less than £50,271** who aren't in an eligible public service job. The consultation floated **15 years** for jobs below RQF 6, but that idea was dropped, so they get the same **10 years** as everyone else.
- People on **Family and Private Life** routes, who can't use the 3- or 5-year routes.
- **Care workers** who started on the route **after May 2025**.
- **Skilled Workers already on the route before April 2024** in jobs below RQF 6. They also move to **10 years**, but can go on **extending their visas on their current salary terms until 2035** (the earlier date was 2030), which gives them time to reach settlement.

The 10-year route asks for earnings of at least **£12,571** in 5 of the 7 years before you apply. **Couples can pool their income** against a household threshold of **£25,142**, so a partner working part-time around childcare isn't penalised. Children, people aged 65 or over, and people unable to work because of a physical or mental condition don't have to meet the earnings rule.

## ILR requirements under the new rules

Every route comes with the same baseline. **A higher salary can't make up for missing any of these** (Home Office, Introduction and Chapter 1):

- **English at B2** (upper intermediate), up from today's B1. Reaching C1 will **not** shorten your wait.
- **A pass in the [Life in the UK test](https://www.gov.uk/life-in-the-uk-test)**, which is under review.
- **No UK criminal record.** Adults with any UK criminal record, court fines, conditional cautions and suspended sentences included, will be refused.
- **No debts to the government**, which covers **NHS debt**, **unpaid tax and National Insurance**, and Home Office legal costs.
- **No specified public funds in the 5 years before you apply.** Claiming them puts you on the **20-year** route, unless you then go 5 straight years without claiming.
- **No immigration breaches as an adult**, such as **overstaying** or **working against your visa conditions**. These also put you on the **20-year** route.

## Students and Graduate visa holders: time will not count

For international students, this is the change that matters most. Right now, years on a **Student** or **Graduate** visa can count towards the **10-year Long Residence** route. Under Earned Settlement:

- the **Long Residence route closes**;
- time on **Student, Graduate, Visit, Youth Mobility, Seasonal Worker, Global Business Mobility** and other temporary routes **won't count** towards settlement;
- your settlement clock only starts once you move onto a route that counts, such as the **Skilled Worker visa**, a family route or a talent route.

(Home Office, Chapter 4)

## Partners and children

Families come out better in the final plan than in the consultation (Home Office, Chapter 5):

- **A partner who comes with you** can settle **alongside you**, if they meet the requirements.
- **A partner who joins you later** serves the same qualifying period. If they arrive 2 years after you on a 5-year route, they apply 2 years after you.
- **Children under 18** can settle with their parents without meeting the income, English or Life in the UK rules.
- **Young adults aged 18 to 24** who are still dependants can settle with the family without meeting the earnings rule.
- **From age 25**, a dependant who hasn't settled with the family has to qualify on their own or move to their own visa.

## Worked examples

How the confirmed rules would play out once they take effect (drawn from the Home Office response; individual cases can differ):

| Your situation | Likely route |
|---|---|
| Skilled Worker on **£38,000**, not in a public service job | **10 years** (at least £12,571 needed in 5 of the 7 years before applying) |
| Skilled Worker on **£55,000** in 3 of the last 5 years | **5 years** (over £50,271) |
| **Nurse or teacher** on a national pay scale | **5 years** (eligible public service job) |
| Earned **£130,000** in 3 of the last 4 years | **3 years** (over £125,141) |
| 3 years on a **Student** visa, 2 on a **Graduate** visa, then a Skilled Worker visa | Student and Graduate time **doesn't count**: the clock starts on the Skilled Worker visa |
| **Care worker** who joined before May 2025, earning £25,000+ | **5 years** (transitional protection) |
| Claimed specified public funds in the last 5 years | **20 years**, unless you then go 5 straight years without claiming |

## Who is not affected

These groups keep their own arrangements (Home Office, Chapters 4 and 6):

- the **EU Settlement Scheme**;
- **Hong Kong BN(O)** (5 years) and the **Afghan schemes**;
- **Windrush** cases;
- **bereaved partners** and **victims of domestic abuse**, who can still apply immediately;
- children in care, and young people who grew up in the UK.

The reforms change how you **qualify** for settlement. Nothing in the Home Office response proposes removing ILR from people who already hold it.

## What you should do now

1. **Find your route:** look at your visa type, your earnings over the last few years, and whether your job appears on the public service list.
2. **Already eligible under today's 5-year rules?** Talk to a **regulated immigration adviser** about when to apply. gov.uk has a tool to [find one](https://www.gov.uk/find-an-immigration-adviser).
3. **Keep your paperwork:** P60s, payslips and tax records for every year, because earnings will be checked across 5 to 7 years.
4. **Start preparing for B2 English and the Life in the UK test** now if you'll need them.
5. **Pay off any NHS or tax debt**, and keep to your visa conditions.
6. **Watch the official source.** The changes will be published on gov.uk as a "Statement of changes to the Immigration Rules". Be wary of agents or social media posts that sound certain before the rules are out.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is ILR now 10 years in the UK? Is it confirmed?** It is confirmed as the new standard but **not yet in force**. The Home Secretary said the first changes would be laid in the December Immigration Rules, with the reforms rolling out through 2027 ([EIN, 2 Nov 2026](https://www.ein.org.uk/news/home-secretary-confirms-major-changes-indefinite-leave-remain-rules-under-earned-settlement)).

**Can I still get ILR in 5 years on a Skilled Worker visa?** Yes, if you earn **£50,271** or more in 3 of the 5 years before applying, work in an eligible public service job, have a British partner, or are a care, health or education worker covered by the transitional rules. For everyone else the standard becomes 10 years.

**Will the ILR changes affect people already in the UK? Is it retrospective?** In general, yes, for anyone who doesn't have ILR yet. The government says "settlement rules will otherwise continue to apply at the point of application" ([gov.uk, 2 Nov 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/home-secretary-unveils-new-rules-for-migrants-settling-in-the-uk)), and applications are normally decided under the rules in force on the decision date. Only certain groups are protected: some care, health and education workers who arrived before May 2025 keep 5 years, and pre-April 2024 Skilled Workers in roles below RQF 6 can keep extending on current terms until 2035.

**Does Student or Graduate visa time count towards ILR?** Not under the new system. Temporary routes won't count, and the 10-year Long Residence route is closing.

**Is the Long Residence route closing?** Yes. The government has confirmed it closes under Earned Settlement.

**How much is the ILR fee?** Under the current rules, a Skilled Worker ILR application costs £3,226 per person, with no extra charge for biometrics. A decision usually comes within 6 months, and applicants aged 18 to 64 must pass the Life in the UK test ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/indefinite-leave-to-remain-tier-2-t2-skilled-worker-visa)). Fees normally change every April.

**What English level will I need for ILR?** **B2**, up from B1, unless you're exempt (children, people aged 65 and over, or people with a relevant physical or mental condition).

**Does my partner need to earn £12,571?** Not necessarily. Couples can combine their income to reach **£25,142** between them.

**I already have ILR. Am I affected?** No. In the government's words, "settled status will never be removed from anyone who already holds it" ([gov.uk, 2 Nov 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/home-secretary-unveils-new-rules-for-migrants-settling-in-the-uk)).

**Is ILR the same as EU settled status?** Settled status under the EU Settlement Scheme is a type of indefinite leave for EU citizens and their families who lived in the UK before 2021. That scheme keeps its own rules and isn't touched by Earned Settlement.
