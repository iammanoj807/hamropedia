UK immigration rules for students have changed a lot since the government's May 2025 immigration white paper. This page separates **what is already confirmed** from **what was only proposed**, with the source for each number, so you can plan properly.

> **Update (1 November 2026):** The UK Budget of 28 October 2026 says graduates of **"high-compliance" universities** will keep a **two-year Graduate visa** after January 2027. This is announced policy; the qualifying universities have not been named yet. [Read the full story](/uk-graduate-visa-two-years-high-compliance-universities/).
>
> This article was also fully rewritten in November 2026. An earlier version presented some white-paper proposals as if they were already law; that has been corrected.

## The short version

| Rule | What applies | Since |
|---|---|---|
| Graduate Route length (bachelor's, master's) | **18 months**, for applications made on or after 1 January 2027 (2 years before that) | Confirmed Oct 2025 |
| Graduate Route, **high-compliance universities** | **2 years** even after January 2027 (Budget policy; details and university list not yet published) | Announced 28 Oct 2026 |
| Graduate Route length (PhD) | 3 years (unchanged) | — |
| Student visa application fee | **£558** | 8 April 2026 |
| Immigration Health Surcharge (students) | **£776 per year** | 6 February 2024 |
| Maintenance funds (London) | **£1,529 per month**, up to 9 months | 11 November 2025 |
| Maintenance funds (outside London) | **£1,171 per month**, up to 9 months | 11 November 2025 |
| Bringing dependants | Only for postgraduate research courses (or some government-funded scholarships) | Courses starting from January 2024 |

## 1. The Graduate Route is getting shorter

The **Graduate Route** lets you stay and work in the UK after finishing a UK degree, without a job offer. The Home Office confirmed in its October 2025 Statement of Changes that it will be cut:

- **Bachelor's and master's graduates:** 18 months instead of 2 years.
- **PhD and other doctoral graduates:** still 3 years.
- **The cutoff is your application date.** It applies to Graduate Route applications **made on or after 1 January 2027**. Applications made before then get the current 2 years ([UKCISA, 14 October 2025](https://www.ukcisa.org.uk/news/student-update-changes-to-the-student-and-graduate-rules/)).

**New in the October 2026 Budget:** the government says graduates of **"high-compliance universities"** with strong job outcomes will get **two years** instead of 18 months. "High-compliance" has not been defined and no list of universities has been published, so treat it as a promise until it appears in the Immigration Rules. [Details here](/uk-graduate-visa-two-years-high-compliance-universities/).

**What this means for you:** if your course ends in late 2026, you may still be able to apply before 1 January 2027 and get 2 years, as long as you meet the other requirements (including your university reporting that you completed the course). Check with your university's international student advice team.

## 2. Visa fee and health surcharge

- **Student visa fee:** £558, from 8 April 2026, whether you apply from outside the UK or extend/switch inside the UK (it was £524 before).
- **Immigration Health Surcharge (IHS):** £776 per year for students, paid up front for the whole visa length.

So a student on a one-year master's typically pays the £558 visa fee plus the IHS for the length of the visa, which runs a few months beyond the course. Use the official [visa fees calculator](https://www.gov.uk/visa-fees) and the [IHS guidance and calculator](https://www.gov.uk/healthcare-immigration-application/how-much-pay) for your exact figure.

## 3. How much money you need to show

From **11 November 2025** the maintenance (living costs) requirement for new Student visa applications is:

- **£1,529 per month** if you study in London;
- **£1,171 per month** elsewhere in the UK;

for up to **9 months**. You usually also need to show any unpaid first-year tuition fees. The money must normally be held for **28 consecutive days** before you apply ([UKCISA](https://www.ukcisa.org.uk/news/student-update-changes-to-the-student-and-graduate-rules/); [gov.uk: Student visa, money](https://www.gov.uk/student-visa/money)).

**Example:** a 12-month master's outside London needs 9 × £1,171 = **£10,539** for living costs, plus remaining tuition fees.

## 4. Dependants

For courses starting from **January 2024**, most international students **cannot bring family members** (dependants). The exceptions are students on **postgraduate research** programmes such as a PhD, and some government-funded scholarship holders ([gov.uk: Student visa, family members](https://www.gov.uk/student-visa/family-members)).

## 5. English language

You need to prove English at **CEFR level B2** for a degree-level course (B1 below degree level), through an approved test or a university assessment, depending on your sponsor ([gov.uk: Student visa, knowledge of English](https://www.gov.uk/student-visa/knowledge-of-english)).

## 6. Working while you study

On a degree-level course you can usually work up to **20 hours a week during term time**, and full time during official vacations, unless your CAS or visa says otherwise ([gov.uk: Student visa, what you can and cannot do](https://www.gov.uk/student-visa)).

## What was proposed but is not (yet) a student rule

The [May 2025 immigration white paper](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/restoring-control-over-the-immigration-system-white-paper) proposed several other changes, including:

- **stricter compliance thresholds** for universities that sponsor international students;
- a **levy on universities' income from international students**;
- making **10 years**, rather than 5, the normal qualifying period for settlement (permanent residence), with ways to qualify faster. **Update (2 November 2026):** the government has now confirmed this, starting from December 2026 and through 2027. Student and Graduate visa time will not count towards settlement. See [UK ILR changes: 5-year vs 10-year routes](/ilr-changes-uk-5-year-vs-10-year-route-earned-settlement/).

Some of these need further legislation or consultation, and details change. **Treat them as proposals until they appear in the official Immigration Rules or on gov.uk.**

## Checklist before you apply

- [ ] CAS from a licensed sponsor.
- [ ] Maintenance funds held for 28 days, at the new monthly rates.
- [ ] English test or evidence your sponsor accepts.
- [ ] TB test certificate (required for applicants from Nepal; see [gov.uk TB testing](https://www.gov.uk/tb-test-visa)).
- [ ] Visa fee (£558) and IHS (£776 per year) budgeted.
- [ ] Preparation for a possible [credibility interview](/top-ukvi-interview-question-examples-and-answers/).
