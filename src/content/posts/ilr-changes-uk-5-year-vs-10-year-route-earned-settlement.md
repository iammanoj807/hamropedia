The UK government has confirmed the **new ILR rules**, its biggest change to **indefinite leave to remain (ILR)** in years. On **2 November 2026** the Home Office published its final plan, called **"Earned Settlement"**: the standard wait for settlement will rise from **5 years to 10 years**, with shorter routes for people who earn more, work in key public services or have a British partner, and longer routes for people who claim benefits or break immigration rules ([Home Office, Government Response, Nov 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6ae89fddf4a6b3f59cf37580/FINAL_VERSION_-_Earned_Settlement_-_Consultation_Response.pdf)).

> **At a glance**
>
> - **New standard wait for ILR:** **10 years**, up from 5 for most work routes.
> - **Still 5 years:** people earning at least **£50,271**, eligible doctors, nurses, teachers and other public service workers, and partners, parents and children of **British citizens**.
> - **3 years:** people earning at least **£125,141**.
> - **20 years:** people who have claimed specified public funds in the last 5 years, or broken immigration rules as an adult.
> - **Student and Graduate visa time** will no longer count, and the **10-year Long Residence route will close**.
> - **When:** not in force yet. The first changes come in the **December Immigration Rules**, with the rest during **2027**.

## What is indefinite leave to remain (ILR)?

**ILR (indefinite leave to remain)**, also called **settlement**, lets you live in the UK permanently, with no visa to renew, no limits on work, and access to public funds. It is also the usual step before applying for **British citizenship** ([gov.uk: Settle in the UK](https://www.gov.uk/settle-in-the-uk)).

Today, most people on routes like the **Skilled Worker visa** can apply for ILR after **5 years**, and anyone with **10 years of continuous lawful residence** can apply through the **Long Residence** route ([gov.uk: Long residence](https://www.gov.uk/long-residence)). Both are changing.

### "ILR 10 years": two different things

If you search for "ILR 10 years", you will see two different rules mixed together:

- **The old 10-year Long Residence route** (gov.uk's [long residence](https://www.gov.uk/long-residence) page): settlement after 10 years of continuous lawful residence on **any mix of visas**, including Student and Graduate time. It still applies today, but **it will close** under Earned Settlement.
- **The new 10-year standard qualifying period**: the default wait for ILR on routes like the Skilled Worker visa, up from 5 years, with **only time on a qualifying route counting**.

So 10 years on a student visa followed by work will no longer get you settlement on its own. What counts is the time on a route that leads to settlement.

## ILR new rules: the 3, 5, 10 and 20-year routes

| How long | Who it is for | What you need |
|---|---|---|
| **3 years** | Exceptional economic contribution | Earn at least **£125,141** in 3 of the last 4 years, or qualify through the "Highest Talent Group" (still under review) |
| **5 years** | Strong contribution | Earn at least **£50,271** in 3 of the last 5 years, **or** work in an eligible public service job for 5 years, **or** have a British connection |
| **10 years** | The new standard route | Earn at least **£12,571** in 5 of the 7 years before applying, and meet all the basic requirements |
| **20 years** | People who cannot use a shorter route | For example, if you claimed specified public funds in the last 5 years, or broke immigration rules as an adult |

(Home Office, Table 1)

The earnings thresholds are tied to the UK income tax bands (£12,571 is where basic-rate tax starts, £50,271 higher rate, £125,141 additional rate), so they will move if those bands change.

## Who still gets ILR after 5 years

- **Higher earners:** at least **£50,271** a year in 3 of the 5 years before applying. Two lower years are allowed, for example for maternity leave or illness. The 3- and 5-year earnings routes are for people on the **Skilled Worker, Global Talent, Scale-up, Innovator Founder** and **International Sportsperson** routes.
- **Public service workers in health and education:** eligible roles paid on **national pay scales**, such as **doctors, nurses and teachers**. The full list is in Annex C of the Home Office response.
- **A British connection:** eligible **partners, parents and children of British citizens**, and **UK Ancestry** visa holders.
- **Groups with existing promises:** **Hong Kong BN(O)** visa holders keep 5 years. **HM Armed Forces**, including **Gurkha** and Hong Kong military unit veterans discharged before 1 July 1997, keep their existing arrangements: service personnel qualify after at least 4 years' service, and their partners and children after 5 years.

### Transitional protection for some people already in the UK

These apply only to people who were in the route **before the Immigration White Paper of May 2025**:

- **Care workers** who entered or switched into the care worker route before May 2025 keep **5 years**, if they meet the **£25,000** salary, sponsorship and work requirements. Those who don't can apply for a further **five-year Health and Care visa** to try again.
- **Health and education workers below degree level (RQF 6)** on national pay scales, such as **nursing auxiliaries and teaching assistants**, keep a **5-year** route.

(Home Office, Chapter 8)

## Who moves to 10 years

Almost everyone else on a route to settlement, including:

- **Skilled Workers** earning **under £50,271** who are not in an eligible public service job. The consultation had proposed **15 years** for jobs below RQF 6; that was dropped, so they get **10 years** like everyone else.
- People on **Family and Private Life** routes, who cannot use the 3- or 5-year routes.
- **Care workers** who joined the route **after May 2025**.
- **Skilled Workers who were on the route before April 2024**, in roles below RQF 6: they move to **10 years** too, but they can keep **extending their visas on their current salary terms until 2035** (previously 2030), so they have time to reach settlement.

To qualify after 10 years you must earn at least **£12,571** in 5 of the 7 years before applying. **Couples can combine their income**, with a household threshold of **£25,142**, so a partner who works part-time while caring for children is not held back. Children, people aged 65 and over, and people who cannot work because of a physical or mental condition are exempt from the earnings rule.

## ILR requirements under the new rules

Whatever your route, you will need to meet these minimum requirements. **Earning more cannot make up for them** (Home Office, Introduction and Chapter 1):

- **English at B2 level** (upper intermediate), up from B1 today. A higher level (C1) will **not** shorten your wait.
- **Pass the [Life in the UK test](https://www.gov.uk/life-in-the-uk-test)**, which is being reviewed.
- **No criminal record in the UK.** Adults with any UK criminal record, including court fines, conditional cautions and suspended sentences, will be refused.
- **No money owed to the government**, including **NHS debt**, **unpaid tax and National Insurance**, and Home Office legal costs.
- **No specified public funds in the 5 years before applying.** If you have claimed them, you are on the **20-year** route, unless you then go 5 years in a row without claiming.
- **No immigration breaches as an adult**, such as **overstaying** or **working in breach of your visa conditions**. These also move you to the **20-year** route.

## Students and Graduate visa holders: time will not count

This is the biggest change for international students. Today, years on a **Student** or **Graduate** visa can count towards the **10-year Long Residence** route. Under Earned Settlement:

- the **Long Residence route will close**;
- time on **Student, Graduate, Visit, Youth Mobility, Seasonal Worker, Global Business Mobility** and other temporary routes will **not count** towards settlement;
- your settlement clock starts when you move onto a route that counts, such as the **Skilled Worker visa**, a family route or a talent route.

(Home Office, Chapter 4)

If you are studying now, see what is changing with the [UK Graduate visa](/uk-graduate-visa-two-years-high-compliance-universities/) and the [UK student visa rules](/uk-immigration-2025-new-student-visa-rules-explained-simply/).

## Partners and children

The final plan is kinder to families than the consultation (Home Office, Chapter 5):

- **A partner who arrives with you** can settle **at the same time** as you, if they meet the requirements.
- **A partner who joins later** completes the same qualifying period. For example, if they join 2 years after you on a 5-year route, they can apply 2 years after you.
- **Children under 18** can settle with their parents and don't need to meet the income, English or Life in the UK requirements.
- **Young adults aged 18 to 24** who are still dependants can settle with their family without meeting the earnings rule.
- **From age 25**, a dependant who has not yet settled with their family must qualify in their own right or move to their own visa.

## Worked examples

How the confirmed rules would apply, once in force (based on the Home Office response; your own case may differ):

| Your situation | Likely route |
|---|---|
| Skilled Worker earning **£38,000**, not in a public service job | **10 years** (you need at least £12,571 in 5 of the 7 years before applying) |
| Skilled Worker earning **£55,000** in 3 of the last 5 years | **5 years** (above £50,271) |
| **Nurse or teacher** paid on a national pay scale | **5 years** (eligible public service job) |
| Earned **£130,000** in 3 of the last 4 years | **3 years** (above £125,141) |
| 3 years on a **Student** visa, 2 on a **Graduate** visa, then a Skilled Worker visa | Student and Graduate time **doesn't count**: your clock starts on the Skilled Worker visa |
| **Care worker** who joined before May 2025, earning £25,000+ | **5 years** (transitional protection) |
| Claimed specified public funds in the last 5 years | **20 years**, unless you then go 5 years in a row without claiming |

## Who is not affected

These keep their own arrangements (Home Office, Chapters 4 and 6):

- the **EU Settlement Scheme**;
- **Hong Kong BN(O)** (5 years) and **Afghan schemes**;
- **Windrush** cases;
- **bereaved partners** and **victims of domestic abuse**, who can still apply straight away;
- children in care, and young people who have grown up in the UK.

The plan is about how people **qualify** for settlement. The Home Office response does not propose taking ILR away from people who already have it.

## ILR application fee and decision time (today)

Under the current rules, applying for ILR after a Skilled Worker visa costs **£3,226 per person**. Giving your fingerprints and photo is free, and you usually get a decision **within 6 months** of doing so. If you are aged 18 to 64 you must pass the Life in the UK test, and you must not travel outside the UK, Ireland, the Channel Islands or the Isle of Man until you get a decision ([gov.uk: ILR for Skilled Workers](https://www.gov.uk/indefinite-leave-to-remain-tier-2-t2-skilled-worker-visa)). Fees usually change each April, so check gov.uk before you apply.

## When do the changes start?

**Not yet.** The new system has been announced but is **not law** until it appears in the Immigration Rules. In her statement to Parliament, the Home Secretary said the first changes will be laid in the **December Immigration Rules**, and the reforms will be brought in **over the course of 2027** ([EIN, 2 Nov 2026](https://www.ein.org.uk/news/home-secretary-confirms-major-changes-indefinite-leave-remain-rules-under-earned-settlement)). More guidance is due before then.

One point matters for everyone already in the UK. The government says that, unless transitional rules say otherwise, settlement applications are normally decided under **the rules in force on the date of the decision**, not the rules when you arrived (Home Office, Chapter 8). Apart from the groups listed above, there is **no general protection** for people who are already here.

## What you should do now

1. **Work out which route fits you:** your visa type, your salary over the last few years, and whether your job is on the public service list.
2. **If you already qualify for ILR under today's 5-year rules,** speak to a **regulated immigration adviser** about timing. You can [find one on gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/find-an-immigration-adviser).
3. **Keep your records:** P60s, payslips and tax records for every year. Earnings will be checked over 5 to 7 years.
4. **Start on B2 English and the Life in the UK test** now if you will need them.
5. **Clear any NHS or tax debt**, and stay within your visa conditions.
6. **Follow the official source.** Changes will appear on gov.uk in a "Statement of changes to the Immigration Rules". Be careful with agents or social media posts that promise certainty before the rules are published.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is ILR now 10 years in the UK? Is it confirmed?** It has been confirmed as the new standard, but it is **not in force yet**. The changes start in the December Immigration Rules and roll out over 2027.

**Can I still get ILR in 5 years on a Skilled Worker visa?** Yes, if you earn at least **£50,271** in 3 of the 5 years before applying, work in an eligible public service job, have a British partner, or are a care, health or education worker covered by the transitional rules. Otherwise the standard will be 10 years.

**Will the ILR changes affect people already in the UK? Is it retrospective?** Generally yes, for people who do not have ILR yet. The government says "settlement rules will otherwise continue to apply at the point of application" ([gov.uk, 2 Nov 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/home-secretary-unveils-new-rules-for-migrants-settling-in-the-uk)). Applications are normally decided under the rules in force on the decision date. Only specific groups have transitional protection: some care, health and education workers who arrived before May 2025 keep 5 years, and pre-April 2024 Skilled Workers in roles below RQF 6 can keep extending their visas on current terms until 2035.

**Does Student or Graduate visa time count towards ILR?** Not under the new system. Time on temporary routes will not count, and the 10-year Long Residence route will close.

**Is the Long Residence route closing?** Yes. The government has confirmed it will close under Earned Settlement.

**How much is the ILR fee?** £3,226 per person for a Skilled Worker ILR application, with no extra charge for biometrics ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/indefinite-leave-to-remain-tier-2-t2-skilled-worker-visa)).

**What English level will I need for ILR?** **B2**, up from B1, unless you are exempt (children, people aged 65 and over, or people with a relevant physical or mental condition).

**Does my partner need to earn £12,571?** Not necessarily. Couples can combine income to reach **£25,142** together.

**I already have ILR. Am I affected?** No. The government says "settled status will never be removed from anyone who already holds it" ([gov.uk, 2 Nov 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/home-secretary-unveils-new-rules-for-migrants-settling-in-the-uk)).

**Is ILR the same as EU settled status?** Settled status under the EU Settlement Scheme is a form of indefinite leave for EU citizens and their families who lived in the UK before 2021. The EU Settlement Scheme keeps its own rules and is not affected by Earned Settlement.
