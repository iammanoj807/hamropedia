**Balen Shah (Balendra Shah) took the oath as Nepal's prime minister on 27 March 2026 (Chaitra 13, 2082)**. It was the end point of one of the most turbulent years in the country's modern politics: in little more than six months, Nepal moved from deadly street protests to the largest single-party election victory in decades. Here is the story in order, with a link to the report behind each fact.

## Timeline at a glance

| Date | What happened |
|---|---|
| Early Sept 2025 | The government blocks 26 social media platforms that had not registered with the authorities |
| 8 Sept 2025 (Bhadra 23, 2082) | Youth-led "Gen Z" protests begin; police respond with deadly force |
| 9 Sept 2025 | Prime Minister K. P. Sharma Oli resigns |
| 12 Sept 2025 | Parliament is dissolved; former Chief Justice **Sushila Karki** becomes interim prime minister, the first woman to lead a government in Nepal |
| 5 Mar 2026 | General election for all 275 seats of the House of Representatives |
| Mid-Mar 2026 | Final results: the **Rastriya Swatantra Party (RSP)** wins 182 seats |
| 27 Mar 2026 | **Balen Shah** is sworn in as prime minister |

## How it started: the Gen Z protests

Everything began on 8 September 2025, when young people took to the streets over the government's block on 26 social media platforms. Within days the protests had become a broader uprising against corruption and the established political leaders. **At least 76 people were killed** and thousands hurt, according to figures reported by Reuters and collected on [Wikipedia's election article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Nepalese_general_election). Prime Minister Oli stepped down on 9 September.

Three days later, on 12 September, President Ram Chandra Paudel dissolved the House of Representatives and named former Chief Justice **Sushila Karki** interim prime minister, with an election called for 5 March 2026.

## The March 2026 election

Turnout was about **59% of registered voters**, and the outcome was a landslide for the RSP, which had picked former Kathmandu mayor Balen Shah as its candidate for prime minister:

| Party | Seats (of 275) |
|---|---|
| Rastriya Swatantra Party (RSP) | **182** |
| Nepali Congress | 38 |
| CPN (UML) | 25 |
| Nepali Communist Party | 17 |
| Shram Sanskriti Party | 7 |
| Rastriya Prajatantra Party | 5 |

A majority takes 138 seats. No single party had won one alone since 1999, and the RSP's 47.8% of the proportional vote was the best result since that system was introduced in 2008. Nepali Congress and CPN (UML) both recorded their worst results ever ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Nepalese_general_election), citing Reuters, Al Jazeera and The Guardian).

The race everyone watched was **Jhapa-5**, where Shah ran against Oli directly and beat him. The four-time prime minister lost his own seat ([The Hitavada](https://www.thehitavada.com/Encyc/2026/3/8/balendra-shah-defeats-4-time-pm-oli-rsp-set-to-form-next-govt-in-nepal.html)).

## Who is Balen Shah?

- **Born:** 27 April 1990, in Kathmandu.
- **Education:** a bachelor's in civil engineering (Himalayan WhiteHouse International College) and a master's in structural engineering from NITTE in Bengaluru, India, in 2018.
- **Before politics:** a rapper in Nepal's underground hip-hop scene, and a structural engineer.
- **Mayor of Kathmandu, 2022:** elected as an independent, the first independent ever to hold the post.
- **Party:** formally joined the Rastriya Swatantra Party in late December 2025 and led it into the March 2026 election ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balen_Shah)).

## Prime Minister Balen Shah

President Paudel swore Shah in under Article 76(1) of the Constitution. At 35, he was called Nepal's youngest prime minister in decades, and he is the youngest since Nepal became a federal democratic republic ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balen_Shah)). He is also the first prime minister from the **Madhesh** region ([SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/news/asia/south-asia/article/3348119/balendra-shah-sworn-nepals-prime-minister); [Outlook India](https://www.outlookindia.com/international/nepal-swears-in-balendra-shah-as-youngest-prime-minister)). The Harvard-educated economist **Swarnim Waglé** became finance minister.

## Why it matters

Voters clearly rejected the parties that had run Nepal for decades. With a majority of its own, the RSP has no coalition partners to blame. The real test is whether quick reforms become lasting change on jobs, prices and public services, the very issues that drove young people onto the streets.

## The first seven months in office

- **Reform agenda:** the government launched a **100-point governance reform agenda** and reduced the number of ministries ([Tribune India, one month in](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/world/marred-by-controversy-light-on-reforms-nepals-super-pm-balen-shah-completes-one-month-in-office/)).
- **Mixed reviews:** supporters praise the speed and the tough stance on corruption; the Public Procurement Monitoring Office says **120 firms have been blacklisted since 27 March** ([Nepal News, 30 Oct 2026](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/business/nepal-news-evening-economic-brief-october-30-2026/)). Critics point to two ministers quitting in the first month, a contested demolition drive against riverbank settlements in the Kathmandu Valley, and a prime minister who seldom talks to the press ([The Annapurna Express](https://theannapurnaexpress.com/story/63906/); [Gulf Times, 100 days](https://www.gulf-times.com/article/728679/international/nepal/nepal-pm-marks-100-days-with-fast-change-and-few-words)).
- **Press freedom:** in late October, police visited several newsrooms and took journalists in for questioning over their reporting, including a story about the police chief, which journalists' organisations protested ([OnlineKhabar](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/police-visit-offices-of-multiple-media-outlets-several-journalists-taken-into-custody.html)).
- **The courts:** RSP lawmakers have recommended setting up an impeachment recommendation committee, citing Article 101 on judicial misconduct ([Nepal News, 1 Nov 2026](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/main-left/nepal-news-evening-briefing-sunday-november-01-2026/)).
- **The economy:** public debt has gone past Rs 3 trillion, and the UN has given Nepal until 24 November 2029 to graduate from Least Developed Country status ([Nepal News, 30 Oct 2026](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/business/nepal-news-evening-economic-brief-october-30-2026/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Who is the new (current) prime minister of Nepal?** Balendra "Balen" Shah of the Rastriya Swatantra Party, in office since 27 March 2026.

**When did Balen Shah become prime minister?** President Ram Chandra Paudel swore him in on 27 March 2026 (Chaitra 13, 2082 BS).

**How old is Balen Shah?** Born on 27 April 1990, he is 36; he was 35 when he took office ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balen_Shah)).

**Is Balen Shah the youngest prime minister of Nepal?** He is the youngest since Nepal became a federal democratic republic, and at his swearing-in he was described as the youngest in decades.

**Why is Balen Shah famous?** He first made his name as a rapper in Nepal's hip-hop scene and as a structural engineer. In 2022 he became Kathmandu's first independent mayor, and his profile rose further during the 2025 Gen Z protests.

**When did the Gen Z protests in Nepal happen?** They started on 8 September 2025 (Bhadra 23, 2082), and Prime Minister Oli resigned the following day.

**Why did Gen Z protest in Nepal?** The spark was the government's block on 26 social media platforms. The protests soon turned into an uprising against corruption and the old political leadership.

**How many people died in the Gen Z protests?** At least 76, with thousands injured, according to figures reported by Reuters.
