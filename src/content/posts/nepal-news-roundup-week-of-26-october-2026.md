## 1. Cyclone Arnab brings rain, snow and blocked roads

The remnants of **Cyclone Arnab** brought days of rain to much of the country and heavy snow in the high mountains. On 31 October, Nepal Police reported **more than 12 major road sections blocked**, including:

- **Prithvi Highway** at Parebabhir, Gajuri Rural Municipality, Dhading (blocked both ways);
- **BP Highway** at Pinthali and Gimdibesi, Roshi Rural Municipality, Kavrepalanchowk;
- sections of the **Karnali Highway** (Surkhet), the **Mid-Hill Highway** (Khotang) and the **Kaligandaki Corridor** (Gulmi and Palpa).

In **Upper Mustang**, 2 to 3 feet of snow blocked the **Korala** border trade point and landslides cut the Jomsom–Korala road. Kathmandu had three days of continuous rain, and authorities warned of rising rivers and landslide risk. The weather was expected to ease from the afternoon of 31 October ([Nepal News, 31 Oct](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/main-right/nepal-news-evening-briefing-saturday-october-31-2026/)).

**If you are travelling:** check road status with Nepal Police or local news before setting off, especially on the Prithvi and BP highways.

## 2. Public debt passes Rs 3 trillion

Nepal's outstanding public debt reached **Rs 3,009.11 billion** by mid-October, according to the Public Debt Management Office:

- **foreign debt:** Rs 1,598.80 billion (53.13%);
- **domestic debt:** Rs 1,410.31 billion;
- **new borrowing in the first quarter** of fiscal year 2026/27: Rs 123.32 billion, against an annual target of Rs 658.28 billion.

([Nepal News economic brief, 30 Oct](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/business/nepal-news-evening-economic-brief-october-30-2026/))

## 3. No more antibiotics without a prescription

The health ministry has brought in a five-year **National Action Plan on Antimicrobial Resistance** that **bans retail sales of antibiotics without a prescription**. The plan is budgeted at **Rs 4.58 billion**, most of it for human health (Rs 2.65 billion) and animal health (Rs 1.99 billion). Antibiotic misuse makes infections harder to treat for everyone, so this is one of the more significant public-health changes of the year ([Nepal News, 31 Oct](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/main-right/nepal-news-evening-briefing-saturday-october-31-2026/)).

## 4. Less than half of Nepal has safe drinking water

A National Statistics Office report produced with UN-Habitat found that only **47.5%** of people have safely managed drinking water and **39.9%** have safely managed sanitation. *E. coli* was found in **61.2%** of household drinking-water samples (same source).

## 5. UN extends Nepal's LDC graduation deadline to 2029

The UN General Assembly has extended the deadline for **Nepal and Bangladesh** to graduate from **Least Developed Country (LDC)** status to **24 November 2029**, giving more time to prepare for losing trade benefits that come with LDC status ([Nepal News economic brief, 30 Oct](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/business/nepal-news-evening-economic-brief-october-30-2026/)).

## 6. Kathmandu power cuts until 5 November

The Nepal Electricity Authority announced **daily power cuts of 5.5 hours (10:30 am to 4:00 pm)** in Kathmandu from **29 October to 5 November** (same source).

## 7. Press freedom and the courts

After incidents in which police entered newsrooms and questioned journalists, the ruling **RSP** directed the government to investigate. The party also discussed pursuing parliamentary impeachment as part of what it calls judicial restructuring ([Nepal News, 31 Oct](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/main-right/nepal-news-evening-briefing-saturday-october-31-2026/)). For the background, read [how Nepal got here, from the Gen Z protests to PM Balen Shah](/nepal-gen-z-protests-to-balen-shah-government-explained/).

## 8. Nepal and Bhutan take a mountain agenda to COP31

Ahead of the **COP31** climate summit in Antalya, Türkiye (9–20 November), Nepal and Bhutan jointly asked for a mountain climate agenda. They cited this year's **Bhote Koshi flood**, which caused an estimated **USD 2.7 billion** in damage, with reconstruction needs of **USD 4.7 billion**, about 10% of GDP (same source).
